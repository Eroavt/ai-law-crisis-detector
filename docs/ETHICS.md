# Ethics Statement — AI-Law Crisis Detector (ALDC)

## Purpose of this document

This file documents the ethical design choices of the ALDC artifact accompanying the paper **"Duty, Defect, and Disclosure: Reassessing Developer Liability for LLM Chatbots in Suicidal Crises under the EU and Swiss Law"** (University of Zurich, *AI: Technology and Law*, FS26). It is referenced from §2 of the paper's *Technical and Legal Analysis* and serves as the ethics annex required of any research artifact that produces, analyses, or evaluates suicide-related content.

## Use of synthetic data

The artifact is built on a corpus of **synthetic dialogues** generated by Claude Sonnet 4.6 under tightly parametric prompting. We chose synthetic over real for four reasons:

1. **Privacy and dignity.** Real chat logs of users in suicidal crisis cannot be lawfully or ethically collected without explicit, informed consent from people in the worst moments of their lives. We will not seek such consent for a Master's-level research artifact.
2. **Legal exposure of real-data collection.** Under GDPR Art. 9 / nDSG Art. 5 lit. c, real conversations of this type are sensitive health-related personal data and require an Art. 9(2) / Art. 6(1) lawful basis we cannot establish for a course project.
3. **Reproducibility.** Synthetic data with disclosed generation prompts is reproducible by any reader who has API access. Real data cannot be redistributed.
4. **Coverage of legal stress tests.** Real data is sparse on the specific failure modes the paper argues about (the *Raine* knowledge-seeking trap, the *Eliza* multi-turn drift, the *Setzer* anthropomorphic dependence). Synthetic generation lets us cover the legal axes exhaustively.

The synthetic data is for **evaluation only — never used for training**. We have not fine-tuned any model on this corpus and will not release a fine-tuned model based on it.

## Generation safeguards

The corpus-generation prompt (`src/aldc/prompts/corpus_generator.txt`) instructs the model:

- **No real names, addresses, or identifying details.** All personas are generic.
- **No specific lethal doses, step-by-step methods, or named medications with quantities.** The user side of a `method_availability` dialogue may *request* such information, but the assistant turns and the dialogue body do not *state* it. The test is whether downstream systems handle the request appropriately, not how to do harm.
- **No glorification or romanticisation** of suicide. The user voice is realistic and human, not theatrical.
- **No sexualised content for minor characters.** Vulnerability scenarios involving minors stay age-appropriate.
- **Realistic Swiss/European context where natural** (CHF, German/French/Italian first names, EU geography), but not forced.

The complete prompt is checked into version control and is auditable. Any deviation between the prompt and the produced corpus is detectable from `data/corpus.jsonl`.

## Disclosure of AI assistance (UZH §4.5 compliance)

Per the UZH Faculty of Law *Guidelines for Academic Essays* §4.5 and the originality declaration §7, we disclose all AI tool usage that produced text, data, code, or content in the artifact or the paper:

- **Synthetic corpus generation.** Claude Sonnet 4.6 (claude-sonnet-4-6) was used to produce every dialogue in `data/corpus.jsonl` and `data/corpus_de|fr|it.jsonl`. The system prompt and the user prompt are checked into `src/aldc/prompts/corpus_generator.txt` and the generation parameters are recorded per-call in the JSONL `generation_notes` field. Generated under the user's Claude Max subscription via `claude -p` and (where applicable) the official Anthropic API.
- **Detection ("the rater")**. Claude Opus 4.7 (claude-opus-4-7) is the calibrated rater that classifies each conversation against the C-SSRS and ASQ instruments. The system prompt is in `src/aldc/prompts/detector_system.txt`. Two independent passes per conversation produce a Cohen's κ.
- **Baseline continuations.** Claude Sonnet 4.6 produces the "naive", "policy-only", and "detector-wrapped" assistant continuations for the side-by-side comparison.
- **Adversarial user simulator.** Claude Sonnet 4.6, with `src/aldc/prompts/user_simulator.txt`, plays the user side of the multi-turn guardrail-decay probes.
- **Live-provider scorecard (Day 3 work).** Where the artifact tests live commercial providers — OpenAI gpt-4o family and Google gemini-2.5 family — those calls go through their respective public APIs at the rate documented in `docs/REPRODUCE.md`.

**Prose drafting in the paper.** AI tools were not used to draft the paper's legal prose. AI was used (a) as a brainstorming and outlining partner, (b) for sentence-level grammar polishing, and (c) for footnote / citation formatting. Every legal claim is sourced to primary law (Fedlex, EUR-Lex), a peer-reviewed article, or a published commentary, listed in the bibliography. The technical-tools list in the paper's appendix follows the UZH §4.5 format.

## Workshop demonstration

When demonstrating the artifact live at the workshop on 22 May 2026, we will:

- Not reproduce verbatim any specific lethal-means content from the corpus, even on screen. Slides redact dose figures and method names.
- Use a content warning at the start of the talk.
- Identify the local crisis hotlines visible during the demo (Switzerland: 143 Dargebotene Hand; 147 Pro Juventute; 144 emergency medical; 112 EU emergency).

## Data retention

The synthetic corpus, detection results, and baseline continuations are stored only in this repository and in the submission package on OLAT. They are not uploaded to any third-party dataset hub except, after the paper is graded and with the supervisor's approval, possibly Zenodo with a clear research-only license.

## Conflict of interest

The authors declare no financial conflict of interest with Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Character.AI, or Chai. The choice to use Claude (Anthropic) as the detector is methodologically justified by the published zero-shot C-SSRS benchmark (Levkovich et al., *arXiv:2505.13480*, 2025) showing Claude as the strongest off-the-shelf rater on this task.
